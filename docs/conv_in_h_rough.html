Convergence in 𝘩 with rough forcing

Last modified: 30-10-2026.

Go back to the Contents page.

1 Problem and exact solution

On the tadpole graph with stem length \(1\) and loop length \(2\), let \(L=\kappa^2-\Delta_\Gamma\) and consider \[ \partial_tu+\nu L^{\alpha/2}u=g,\qquad u(0)=0, \qquad T=1,\quad\nu=1,\quad\kappa=4. \] Use the same forcing for every \(\alpha\): \[ g=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1j\phi_j, \qquad \phi_j(s)= \begin{cases} 0,&s\text{ on the stem},\\ \sin(2^j\pi x),&s=x\in[0,2]\text{ on the loop}. \end{cases} \] These orthonormal Kirchhoff eigenfunctions have eigenvalues \(\mu_j=\kappa^2+\pi^2 2^{2j}\). The series defines an \(L_2(\Gamma)\) forcing: \(\|g\|_{L_2(\Gamma)}^2=\sum_{j\geq1}j^{-2}<\infty\). For every \(s>0\), \(\sum_{j\geq1}\mu_j^s/j^2=\infty\), so \(g\) has no positive spectral Sobolev regularity. Writing \(a_j=\nu\mu_j^{\alpha/2}\) gives the exact solution \[ u(s,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-a_jt}}{j\,a_j}\phi_j(s). \]

Theorem lastlasttheorem in main.tex gives the spatial term \(h^{\alpha-\epsilon}\) because \(s_f=0\) and \(u_0=0\) belongs to every spectral Sobolev space. For this particular forcing, uniform P1 meshes with \(h_J=2^{-J}\) give the sharper asymptotic behavior \[ E_{h_J}:=\|u-U_{h_J,m}^{\tau}\|_{L_2(0,T;L_2(\Gamma))} \asymp\frac{h_J^\alpha}{J},\qquad0<\alpha<2, \] when temporal and rational errors are negligible. Thus the raw slope tends to \(\alpha\); plotting \(J E_{h_J}\) removes the leading logarithmic factor.

2 Matrix discretization and error

The consistent P1 mass and stiffness matrices are assembled on the tadpole with MetricGraph. Every backward Euler step advances the FEM coefficient vector by shifted sparse matrix solves, using the same matrix update as cov_in_h_mat.Rmd. The rational inverse uses the operator-based root/factor representation with order \(m=8\). Coefficients are read directly at \(\beta=\alpha/2\) from matlab/computed_fractional_roots.csv; no interpolation in \(\beta\) is used. These locally generated Cheb–Padé coefficients are distinct from the installed rSPDE tables. The degree-\(m\) numerator and degree-\((m+1)\) denominator give \(m+1\) shifted solves and no constant term in the update. Factorizations are reused.

This branch retains the same meshes, diffusion, and time steps as the BRASIL baseline. The coefficient comparison also reports order \(m=4\) and preserves the original BRASIL results.

The forcing is supported on the loop and has exact reflection symmetries. The shifted FEM matrices are restricted to that invariant nodal subspace, and the full graph solution is recovered by signed reflections. This is the same FEM matrix evolution; no discrete eigenfunction recurrence is used.

The load is assembled by analytic sine–hat integrals. On a mesh \(h_J=2^{-J}\), the modes \(j\geq J\) have zero \(L_2\) projection onto \(V_{h_J}\). They remain part of the exact solution and its error. Their squared contribution is \[ E_{\mathrm{tail}}^2= \sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\frac1{j^2a_j^2} \left[T-\frac{2(1-e^{-a_jT})}{a_j} +\frac{1-e^{-2a_jT}}{2a_j}\right]. \] The omitted remainder of this infinite sum is bounded by a geometric series and is at most \(10^{-13}\) of its accumulated value. For the resolved modes, the FEM mass norm, the spatial projection residual, and exact time moments evaluate the error of the right-constant reconstruction on each solver interval. No reference-mesh sampling or finite-mode replacement of \(g\) is used.

The logarithmic correction accounts for the factor \(1/J\) in the ideal spatial error model. The measured error also contains the fixed-order rational approximation error. If that contribution dominates, multiplying by \(J\) cannot restore the ideal spatial slope. The matched coefficient comparison separates that effect from the finite spatial refinement range. For \(\alpha\) close to \(2\), the second-order contribution from low modes can remain significant over this mesh range, so the limiting corrected slope can take longer to appear.
