Fractional diffusion equations on metric graphs

We analyze and numerically approximate solutions to fractional diffusion equations on metric graphs of the form \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:maineq} \tag{1} \left\{ \begin{aligned} \partial_t u(s,t)+\nu(\kappa^2-\Delta_\Gamma)^{\alpha/2}u(s,t) &=f(s,t), && (s,t)\in\Gamma\times(0,T),\\ u(s,0)&=u_0(s), && s\in\Gamma. \end{aligned} \right. \end{equation}\] Here \(\Gamma=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E)\) is a compact connected metric graph, \(\kappa>0\), \(\nu>0\) is a fixed diffusion coefficient, \(\alpha\in(0,2]\) is the fractional diffusion exponent, and \(f\) and \(u_0\) are the right-hand side and initial condition, respectively. The Kirchhoff–Laplacian \(\Delta_\Gamma\) acts edgewise and has domain \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:Kcond} \tag{2} \mathcal D(\Delta_\Gamma)= \left\{\phi\in C(\Gamma):\ \phi|_e\in H^2(e)\ \text{for every }e\in\mathcal E, \quad\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_v}\partial_e\phi(v)=0 \ \text{for every }v\in\mathcal V\right\}. \end{equation}\] The derivatives in the vertex condition are directed from the vertex into the incident edges, with each edge endpoint counted separately. We write \(L=\kappa^2-\Delta_\Gamma\) and \(\beta=\alpha/2\). Fractional powers of \(L\) are defined spectrally; the vertex conditions are incorporated through \(L\). The evolution equation is therefore \(u_t+\nu L^\beta u=f\). The coefficient \(\nu\) multiplies the fractional operator after its power is taken.

Numerical Scheme

Let \(V_h\) be the space of continuous piecewise linear finite element functions on a graph mesh indexed by \(h\), and let \(P_h\) denote the \(L_2(\Gamma)\)-orthogonal projection onto \(V_h\). Define the discrete operator \(L_h:V_h\to V_h\) by \[ (L_hv_h,w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)} =\kappa^2(v_h,w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)} +(v_h',w_h')_{L_2(\Gamma)},\qquad v_h,w_h\in V_h. \] Its fractional power induces the discrete bilinear form \[ \mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}(v_h,w_h) =(L_h^{\alpha/4}v_h,L_h^{\alpha/4}w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)}. \] Thus, the spatial operator is discretized first and its fractional power is then taken on \(V_h\). We keep \(L_h\) and \(\mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}\) defined for the unscaled spatial operator; the diffusion term in the weak equation is \(\nu\mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}\).

Let \(t_k=k\tau\), \(k=0,\ldots,N\), with \(\tau=T/N\). The scheme computes \(U_h^\tau=\{U_h^k\}_{k=0}^{N}\), where \(U_h^k\in V_h\) and \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} \left(\delta U_h^{k+1},\phi\right)_{L_2(\Gamma)} +\nu\mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}(U_h^{k+1},\phi) &=(f^{k+1},\phi)_{L_2(\Gamma)},\qquad \phi\in V_h,\\ U_h^0&=P_hu_0, \end{aligned} \right. \] for \(k=0,\ldots,N-1\), with \[ \delta U_h^{k+1}=\frac{U_h^{k+1}-U_h^k}{\tau}, \qquad f^{k+1}=\frac1\tau\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}f(t)\,dt. \] Equivalently, \[\begin{equation} \label{system:fully_discrete_scheme} \tag{3} \left\{ \begin{aligned} (U_h^{k+1},\phi)_{L_2(\Gamma)} +\tau\nu\mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}(U_h^{k+1},\phi) &=(U_h^k,\phi)_{L_2(\Gamma)} +\tau(f^{k+1},\phi)_{L_2(\Gamma)},\qquad\phi\in V_h,\\ U_h^0&=P_hu_0. \end{aligned} \right. \end{equation}\]

At each time step, the finite element solution can be expressed in the basis \(\{\psi_h^i\}_{i=1}^{N_h}\) as \[\begin{equation} \label{num_sol} \tag{4} U_h^k(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_h}u_i^k\psi_h^i(s),\qquad s\in\Gamma. \end{equation}\] Replacing \(\eqref{num_sol}\) into \(\eqref{system:fully_discrete_scheme}\) yields \[ \sum_{j=1}^{N_h}u_j^{k+1} \left[(\psi_h^j,\psi_h^i)_{L_2(\Gamma)} +\tau\nu\mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}(\psi_h^j,\psi_h^i)\right] =\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}u_j^k(\psi_h^j,\psi_h^i)_{L_2(\Gamma)} +\tau(f^{k+1},\psi_h^i)_{L_2(\Gamma)}, \] for \(i=1,\ldots,N_h\).

Step 1: Represent the discrete operator in the finite element basis

Introduce the mass and stiffness matrices \[ \mathbf C_{i,j}=(\psi_h^i,\psi_h^j)_{L_2(\Gamma)},\qquad \mathbf G_{i,j}=({\psi_h^i}',{\psi_h^j}')_{L_2(\Gamma)},\qquad \mathbf L=\kappa^2\mathbf C+\mathbf G. \] The mass matrix \(\mathbf C\) is symmetric positive definite, and the stiffness matrix \(\mathbf G\) is symmetric positive semidefinite. Hence \(\mathbf L\) is symmetric positive definite because \(\kappa>0\).

To find the matrix representing \(L_h\), write a finite element function and its image as \[ v_h=\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}v_j\psi_h^j, \qquad L_hv_h=\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}z_j\psi_h^j. \] Testing the definition of \(L_h\) with \(w_h=\psi_h^i\) gives \[ \sum_j C_{ij}z_j =\sum_j(\kappa^2 C_{ij}+G_{ij})v_j, \qquad i=1,\ldots,N_h. \] Collecting these equations and solving for the image coefficients yields \[ \mathbf C\mathbf z=\mathbf L\mathbf v, \qquad \mathbf z=\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf L\mathbf v. \] Thus \[ \mathbf B=\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf L \] maps the coefficient vector of \(v_h\) to the coefficient vector of \(L_hv_h\). This is what it means for \(\mathbf B\) to represent \(L_h\) in this basis.

Step 2: Represent the fractional bilinear form

For coefficient vectors \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\), expansion in the basis gives the inner-product identity \[ (v_h,w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)} =\sum_{i,j}w_i v_j(\psi_h^j,\psi_h^i)_{L_2(\Gamma)} =\mathbf w^\top\mathbf C\mathbf v. \] The matrix \(\mathbf B\) is similar to the symmetric positive definite matrix \(\mathbf C^{-1/2}\mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1/2}\): \[ \mathbf B=\mathbf C^{-1/2} (\mathbf C^{-1/2}\mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1/2})\mathbf C^{1/2}. \] It therefore has positive eigenvalues and a spectral fractional power. Applying the same spectral definition to \(L_h\) shows that \(L_h^\beta v_h\) has coefficient vector \(\mathbf B^\beta\mathbf v\). Consequently, \[ \begin{aligned} (L_h^\beta v_h,w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)} &=\sum_{i,j}w_i(\mathbf B^\beta\mathbf v)_j (\psi_h^j,\psi_h^i)_{L_2(\Gamma)}\\ &=\mathbf w^\top\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta\mathbf v. \end{aligned} \] Since \(L_h\) is self-adjoint in the \(L_2(\Gamma)\) inner product and \(\alpha/4=\beta/2\), \[ \begin{aligned} \mathfrak a_{h,\alpha}(v_h,w_h) &=(L_h^{\beta/2}v_h,L_h^{\beta/2}w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)}\\ &=(L_h^\beta v_h,w_h)_{L_2(\Gamma)} =\mathbf w^\top\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta\mathbf v. \end{aligned} \] The matrix of the fractional bilinear form is therefore \(\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta\): the fractional power supplies the image coefficients, and the mass matrix supplies the inner products of the basis functions. Multiplying the fractional operator by \(\nu\) therefore gives the diffusion bilinear-form matrix \(\nu\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta\).

Step 3: Assemble the time-stepping equation

Let \(\mathbf U^k\) have components \(u_i^k\), and let \(\mathbf F^{k+1}\) have components \(F_i^{k+1}=(f^{k+1},\psi_h^i)_{L_2(\Gamma)}\). Choosing \(\phi=\psi_h^i\) in \(\eqref{system:fully_discrete_scheme}\) now gives \[ (\mathbf C\mathbf U^{k+1})_i +\tau\nu(\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta\mathbf U^{k+1})_i =(\mathbf C\mathbf U^k)_i+\tau F_i^{k+1}. \] Since this holds for every \(i\), it is the vector equation \[\begin{equation} \label{diff_eq_discrete} \tag{5} (\mathbf C+\tau\nu\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf C\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}. \end{equation}\] In particular, \(\mathbf C(\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf L)^\beta\) generally differs from the ordinary matrix power \(\mathbf L^\beta\).

Step 4: Remove the mass matrix and normalize the operator

First multiply \(\eqref{diff_eq_discrete}\) on the left by \(\mathbf C^{-1}\). Using \(\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf C=\mathbf I\) gives \[ (\mathbf I+\tau\nu\mathbf B^\beta)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}. \] Next introduce \[ \mathbf A=\frac{\mathbf B}{\kappa^2} =\frac{\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf L}{\kappa^2}. \] The matrices \(\mathbf L\), \(\mathbf B\), and \(\mathbf A\) describe \(L_h\) and its normalization; the scalar \(\nu\) remains outside their fractional powers. In particular, neither their eigenvalues nor the approximation interval \(J_h\) below depends on \(\nu\). Because \(\mathbf B=\kappa^2\mathbf A\) and \(\kappa^2>0\), the spectral definition gives \(\mathbf B^\beta=\kappa^{2\beta}\mathbf A^\beta\). Substitution yields \[ (\mathbf I+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}\mathbf A^\beta)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}. \]

Step 5: Rewrite the scheme using an inverse fractional power

The eigenvalues of \(\mathbf A\) are positive, so \(\mathbf A^{-\beta}\) exists. Multiply the previous equation on the left by this matrix: \[ \mathbf A^{-\beta} (\mathbf I+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}\mathbf A^\beta)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf A^{-\beta} (\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}). \] Using \(\mathbf A^{-\beta}\mathbf A^\beta=\mathbf I\) gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:inverse_scheme} \tag{6} (\mathbf A^{-\beta}+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}\mathbf I)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf A^{-\beta} (\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}). \end{equation}\]

Every step up to this point is an exact rearrangement of the fully discrete fractional scheme. Equation \(\eqref{eq:inverse_scheme}\) prepares us to approximate the inverse power \(\mathbf A^{-\beta}\). The diffusion coefficient enters through \(\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}\). The forcing keeps its factor \(\tau\), because \(\nu\) multiplies only the diffusion operator in the original PDE.

BRASIL rational approximation

Step 6: Locate the eigenvalues to be approximated

Let \(0<\beta<1\), and let \(\lambda_{1,h}\) and \(\lambda_{N_h,h}\) be the smallest and largest eigenvalues of \(L_h\). These are the generalized eigenvalues of \((\mathbf L,\mathbf C)\): \[ \mathbf L\mathbf v_j=\lambda_{j,h}\mathbf C\mathbf v_j, \qquad \mathbf B\mathbf v_j=\lambda_{j,h}\mathbf v_j. \] Since \(\mathbf G\) is positive semidefinite, all \(\lambda_{j,h}\ge\kappa^2\). Constant functions belong to \(V_h\) and have zero derivative, so they give an eigenvalue \(\lambda_{1,h}=\kappa^2\). Normalization and inversion give \[ \mathbf A\mathbf v_j =\frac{\lambda_{j,h}}{\kappa^2}\mathbf v_j, \qquad \mathbf A^{-1}\mathbf v_j =\frac{\kappa^2}{\lambda_{j,h}}\mathbf v_j. \] Taking reciprocals reverses the ordering of these positive eigenvalues. Thus the interval \[ J_h=[\kappa^2\lambda_{N_h,h}^{-1}, \kappa^2\lambda_{1,h}^{-1}]=[\delta_h,1] \] contains the spectrum of \(\mathbf A^{-1}\).

Step 7: Approximate the scalar power on a fixed interval

For the theoretical construction, let \(B_m\) be the best uniform rational approximation of type \((m,m)\) to \(x^\beta\) on \([0,1]\): \[ q_1(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_ix^i,\qquad q_2(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_jx^j,\qquad B_m(x)=\frac{q_1(x)}{q_2(x)}\approx x^\beta. \] Type \((m,m)\) allows both polynomial degrees to be at most \(m\); the best approximation in this setting actually has both degrees exactly \(m\) (Bolin, Mehandiratta, and Simas, 2025, Appendix B, Lemma 14). The BRASIL algorithm computes this approximation numerically, and the implementation below obtains numerical coefficients from rSPDE.

A fixed approximation on \([0,1]\) covers every \(J_h\) and allows the same coefficients to be used on different meshes. The corresponding matrix approximation is \[ \mathbf A^{-\beta}=(\mathbf A^{-1})^\beta \approx B_m(\mathbf A^{-1}). \] Indeed, on an eigenvector of \(\mathbf A^{-1}\) with eigenvalue \(x\), the exact operator multiplies by \(x^\beta\), whereas the rational approximation multiplies by \(B_m(x)\). This explains why approximating the scalar function on an interval containing the spectrum gives a matrix approximation.

Step 8: Change to the normalized operator variable

Set \(y=1/x\), so that \(x^\beta=y^{-\beta}\). Substituting into the rational function and multiplying its numerator and denominator by \(y^m\) gives \[ B_m(y^{-1}) =\frac{q_1(y^{-1})}{q_2(y^{-1})} =\frac{y^m q_1(y^{-1})}{y^m q_2(y^{-1})}. \] Define \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:inverse_rational} \tag{7} S_m(y)=B_m(y^{-1}) =p_\ell(y)^{-1}p_r(y)\approx y^{-\beta}, \qquad p_r(y)=y^m q_1(y^{-1}),\quad p_\ell(y)=y^m q_2(y^{-1}). \end{equation}\] The factors \(y^m\) remove the negative powers, so \(p_r\) and \(p_\ell\) are polynomials. At the matrix level this is the same approximation written as \[ \mathbf A^{-\beta}\approx S_m(\mathbf A) =p_\ell(\mathbf A)^{-1}p_r(\mathbf A). \]

Step 9: Use the proved partial fraction structure

Proposition 3 of Bolin, Mehandiratta, and Simas (2025) applies exactly to this transformed best approximation. In the notation of that proposition, \(P_m=p_r\), \(Q_m=p_\ell\), \(k=d_m\), \(c_j=\rho_j\), and \(p_j=\eta_j\); its exponent \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) is our \(\beta\). It proves the representation \[ S_m(y)=d_m+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_j}{y-\eta_j}, \qquad B_m(x)=d_m+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_jx}{1-\eta_jx}, \] with positive residues and a positive constant, and with negative poles. Lemma 14 and the proof of Proposition 3 establish that the poles are simple and distinct. Ordering them, we therefore have \[ \eta_1<\cdots<\eta_m<0,\qquad \rho_j>0,\qquad d_m>0. \] These properties are consequences of the best-approximation result. The source poles \(\eta_j\), source residues \(\rho_j\), and constant \(d_m\) belong to \(S_m\); the time-step function introduced below will have its own poles and residues.

Each fraction \(\rho_j/(y-\eta_j)\) tends to zero as \(y\to\infty\). Because \(1/y\to0\) and the best approximation has no pole at zero, \[ d_m=B_m(0)=\lim_{y\to\infty}S_m(y)=\frac{c_0}{b_0}. \]

Here the expression for \(B_m(x)\) in partial fractions follows by substituting \(y=1/x\) and simplifying \(1/(x^{-1}-\eta_j)=x/(1-\eta_j x)\), with the value at \(x=0\) defined by continuity.

Step 10: Express the polynomials through their roots

Write the degree-\(m\) polynomials as \[ q_1(x)=c_m\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-r_{1i}),\qquad q_2(x)=b_m\prod_{j=1}^{m}(x-r_{2j}). \] Substituting \(x=y^{-1}\) into the first product gives \[ \begin{aligned} p_r(y)&=y^m q_1(y^{-1}) =c_m y^m\prod_{i=1}^{m}(y^{-1}-r_{1i})\\ &=c_m\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-r_{1i}y). \end{aligned} \] Each factor of \(y\) cancels one factor of \(y^{-1}\). The same calculation for the denominator gives \[ p_r(y)=c_m\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-r_{1i}y),\qquad p_\ell(y)=b_m\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-r_{2j}y). \] Thus, with \(\mathrm{factor}=c_m/b_m\), \[ S_m(y)=\mathrm{factor}\, \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-r_{1i}y)} {\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-r_{2j}y)}. \]

Step 11: Define the matrix products

Replacing \(y\) by \(\mathbf A\) replaces each scalar factor \(1-r y\) by \(\mathbf I-r\mathbf A\). Define \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:PLPR} \tag{8} \mathbf P_r=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\mathbf I-r_{1i}\mathbf A), \qquad \mathbf P_\ell=\frac{\kappa^{2\beta}}{\mathrm{factor}}\, \mathbf C\prod_{j=1}^{m}(\mathbf I-r_{2j}\mathbf A). \end{equation}\] Using the preceding polynomial identities, these definitions are equivalently \[ \mathbf P_r=\frac{p_r(\mathbf A)}{c_m},\qquad \mathbf P_\ell=\frac{\kappa^{2\beta}}{c_m} \mathbf C p_\ell(\mathbf A), \] because \(\mathrm{factor}\,b_m=c_m\). In particular, \(\mathbf P_\ell\) includes the mass matrix and a scaling constant. These choices will give the compact form \(\eqref{eq:scheme}\). Both products are defined independently of \(\nu\); the product scheme therefore carries the diffusion factor explicitly as \(\tau\nu\mathbf P_\ell\). This product representation is algebraic; the partial fraction coefficients allow the same derivation without forming polynomial products.

Derivation of the rational time-stepping scheme

Step 12: Substitute the rational approximation

Replacing \(\mathbf A^{-\beta}\) in \(\eqref{eq:inverse_scheme}\) by \(S_m(\mathbf A)\) gives the rational scheme \[ (S_m(\mathbf A)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}\mathbf I)\mathbf U_m^{k+1} =S_m(\mathbf A) (\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}), \] where \(\mathbf U_m^0\) is the coefficient vector of \(P_hu_0\). The subscript \(m\) distinguishes the rational approximation from the exact discrete fractional scheme. This substitution introduces the rational approximation; the following transformations are algebraic rearrangements of that scheme.

Step 13: Clear the rational denominator

Multiply the rational scheme on the left by \(p_\ell(\mathbf A)\). Since \(p_\ell(\mathbf A)S_m(\mathbf A)=p_r(\mathbf A)\), this gives \[ (p_r(\mathbf A)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(\mathbf A)) \mathbf U_m^{k+1} =p_r(\mathbf A) (\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}). \] Next multiply on the left by \(\mathbf C/c_m\). Using the definitions in \(\eqref{eq:PLPR}\), we obtain \[ (\mathbf C\mathbf P_r+\tau\nu\mathbf P_\ell)\mathbf U_m^{k+1} =\mathbf C\mathbf P_r (\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}). \]

Step 14: Explain the transposes in the product scheme

Symmetry of \(\mathbf C\) and \(\mathbf L\) gives \[ \mathbf A^\top=\frac{\mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1}}{\kappa^2}, \qquad \mathbf A^\top\mathbf C=\frac{\mathbf L}{\kappa^2} =\mathbf C\mathbf A. \] Repeated application yields \((\mathbf A^\top)^j\mathbf C=\mathbf C\mathbf A^j\). Therefore, for any polynomial \(p\) with real coefficients, \[ p(\mathbf A)^\top\mathbf C=\mathbf C p(\mathbf A). \] It follows that \[ \mathbf C\mathbf P_r=\mathbf P_r^\top\mathbf C, \qquad \mathbf P_\ell^\top=\mathbf P_\ell. \] For the second identity, \(\mathbf P_\ell\) is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf C p_\ell(\mathbf A)\), whose transpose is \(p_\ell(\mathbf A)^\top\mathbf C=\mathbf C p_\ell(\mathbf A)\). Substituting these identities into the previous equation and cancelling \(\mathbf C\mathbf C^{-1}\) on its right-hand side gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:scheme} \tag{9} (\mathbf P_r^\top\mathbf C+\tau\nu\mathbf P_\ell^\top) \mathbf U_m^{k+1} =\mathbf P_r^\top(\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}). \end{equation}\] The factors that are polynomials in the same matrix commute; no commutation of \(\mathbf L\) with \(\mathbf C^{-1}\) is assumed.

Step 15: Change to the transposed operator and isolate the solution

Set \[ \mathbf D=\mathbf A^\top =\frac{\mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1}}{\kappa^2}. \] Transposing a polynomial replaces its matrix argument by the transposed matrix: if \(p(\mathbf A)=\sum_j a_j\mathbf A^j\), then \[ p(\mathbf A)^\top =\sum_j a_j(\mathbf A^\top)^j=p(\mathbf D). \] Transposing a product also reverses the order of its factors. Thus \[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf P_r^\top &=\frac{p_r(\mathbf A)^\top}{c_m} =\frac{p_r(\mathbf D)}{c_m},\\ \mathbf P_\ell^\top &=\frac{\kappa^{2\beta}}{c_m} (\mathbf C p_\ell(\mathbf A))^\top\\ &=\frac{\kappa^{2\beta}}{c_m} p_\ell(\mathbf A)^\top\mathbf C^\top =\frac{\kappa^{2\beta}}{c_m}p_\ell(\mathbf D)\mathbf C. \end{aligned} \] Substituting into \(\eqref{eq:scheme}\) and cancelling \(1/c_m\) gives \[ (p_r(\mathbf D)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(\mathbf D)) \mathbf C\mathbf U_m^{k+1} =p_r(\mathbf D)(\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}). \] First multiply on the left by \((p_r(\mathbf D)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(\mathbf D))^{-1}\), and then on the left by \(\mathbf C^{-1}\). The result is \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:final_scheme} \tag{10} \mathbf U_m^{k+1}=\mathbf C^{-1} (p_r(\mathbf D)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(\mathbf D))^{-1} p_r(\mathbf D)(\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}). \end{equation}\] The order of these factors matters: \(\mathbf C^{-1}\) stays to the left of the polynomial inverse. The proved coefficient structure ensures that \(p_\ell\) has only negative zeros and \(S_m(y)>0\) for \(y\ge1\). Consequently \(p_r(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(y) =p_\ell(y)(S_m(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta})\) is nonzero on the spectra of \(\mathbf A\) and \(\mathbf D\), so these inverses are well-defined.

Partial fraction decomposition

Step 16: Introduce the scalar time-step function

Collect the polynomial ratio in \(\eqref{eq:final_scheme}\) into the scalar function \[ H_m(y)=\frac{p_r(y)}{p_r(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(y)}. \] Dividing numerator and denominator by \(p_\ell(y)\) shows that \[ H_m(y)=\frac{S_m(y)}{S_m(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}}. \] At the matrix argument \(\mathbf D\), its meaning is \[ H_m(\mathbf D) =(p_r(\mathbf D)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(\mathbf D))^{-1} p_r(\mathbf D). \] The polynomial factors commute because they are functions of the same matrix. Thus \(\eqref{eq:final_scheme}\) becomes \[ \mathbf U_m^{k+1}=\mathbf C^{-1}H_m(\mathbf D) (\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}). \]

Step 17: Decompose the time-step function and find its constant term

The partial fraction representation is \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:partial_fraction} \tag{11} H_m(y)=\frac{S_m(y)}{S_m(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}} =\frac{p_r(y)}{p_r(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}p_\ell(y)} =\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{a_n}{y-z_n}+r_\tau. \end{equation}\] Here \(z_n\) are the time-step poles and \(a_n\) are their residues. The argument below proves that there are exactly \(m\) simple negative poles, using the coefficient properties established by Proposition 3. Thus the simple-pole expansion follows from the best-approximation structure.

The function \(H_m\) has a nonzero constant term because its numerator and denominator have the same degree. Every fraction in its decomposition vanishes at infinity, so \[ r_\tau=\lim_{y\to\infty}H_m(y) =\lim_{y\to\infty} \frac{S_m(y)}{S_m(y)+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}}. \] Since \(S_m(y)\to d_m\), we obtain \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:remainder} \tag{12} r_\tau=\frac{d_m}{d_m+\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}}. \end{equation}\] This time-step remainder is not the source constant \(d_m\) itself. For the exact best approximation, \(d_m>0\) and \(\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}>0\), so \(0<r_\tau<1\).

Step 18: Evaluate the partial fractions at the matrix argument

Each scalar fraction \(1/(y-z_n)\) becomes a shifted matrix inverse: \[ H_m(\mathbf D)=r_\tau\mathbf I +\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_n(\mathbf D-z_n\mathbf I)^{-1}. \]

Substituting \(\eqref{eq:partial_fraction}\) into \(\eqref{eq:final_scheme}\) gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:final_scheme2} \tag{13} \mathbf U_m^{k+1}=\mathbf C^{-1} \left(\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_n (\mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1}/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf I)^{-1} +r_\tau\mathbf I\right) (\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}). \end{equation}\] #### Step 19: Simplify the shifted solves

Factor each shifted matrix as \[ \mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1}/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf I =(\mathbf L/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf C)\mathbf C^{-1}. \] Inverting a product reverses the order of its factors: \((\mathbf X\mathbf Y)^{-1}=\mathbf Y^{-1}\mathbf X^{-1}\). Consequently, \[ (\mathbf L\mathbf C^{-1}/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf I)^{-1} =\mathbf C(\mathbf L/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf C)^{-1}. \] The outer \(\mathbf C^{-1}\) in \(\eqref{eq:final_scheme2}\) cancels this factor \(\mathbf C\) in each shifted term, whereas the constant term becomes \(r_\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\). We obtain \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:final_scheme3} \tag{14} \mathbf U_m^{k+1} =\mathbf R_{m,\tau}(\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}), \qquad \mathbf R_{m,\tau}=r_\tau\mathbf C^{-1} +\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_n(\mathbf L/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf C)^{-1}. \end{equation}\] Only sparse solves are required: one mass-matrix solve for the constant term when \(r_\tau\ne0\), and one shifted solve for each pole. For fixed \(h\), \(\tau\), \(\nu\), \(\kappa\), \(\beta\), and \(m\), the matrices are unchanged between time steps, so their factorizations can be reused. In practical terms, form \(\mathbf b=\mathbf C\mathbf U_m^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}\), solve \[ \mathbf C\mathbf x_0=\mathbf b,\qquad (\mathbf L/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf C)\mathbf x_n=\mathbf b, \] and combine the solutions as \[ \mathbf U_m^{k+1}=r_\tau\mathbf x_0+\sum_{n=1}^{m}a_n\mathbf x_n. \] This applies the inverses without forming dense inverse matrices.

Poles and residues of the time-step function

Step 20: Identify the scalar equations for the time-step poles

To derive the poles directly from the source partial fractions, set \(s=\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}>0\) and define \[ F_m(y)=S_m(y)+s =d_m+s+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_j}{y-\eta_j}. \] Rewrite the time-step function as \[ H_m(y)=\frac{S_m(y)}{S_m(y)+s} =1-\frac{s}{F_m(y)}. \] The time-step poles \(z_n\) therefore solve \[ F_m(z_n)=d_m+s+\sum_{j=1}^{m} \frac{\rho_j}{z_n-\eta_j}=0. \] Indeed, \(F_m(z_n)=S_m(z_n)+s=0\) implies \[ S_m(z_n)=-s<0. \] The numerator of \(S_m/F_m\) is nonzero at such a point, because \(s>0\); the zero denominator is not cancelled. At a simple zero, locally \[ F_m(y)=F_m'(z_n)(y-z_n)+o(y-z_n),\qquad H_m(y)=1-\frac{s}{F_m'(z_n)(y-z_n)+o(y-z_n)}, \] which exhibits a simple pole. These zeros are exactly the zeros of \(p_r+s p_\ell\): at a source pole \(\eta_j\), the polynomials \(p_r\) and \(p_\ell\) have no common zero, so \((p_r+s p_\ell)(\eta_j)=p_r(\eta_j)\ne0\). Furthermore, as \(y\to\eta_j\), \(S_m(y)\) becomes unbounded and \(H_m(y)=1-s/(S_m(y)+s)\to1\). Thus the source poles are removable singularities of \(H_m\) rather than time-step poles.

Step 21: Prove monotonicity between the source poles

By Proposition 3 and its proof, the ordered source coefficients satisfy \[ \eta_1<\cdots<\eta_m<0,\qquad \rho_j>0,\qquad d_m>0. \] Differentiate \(F_m\) on each real interval away from the source poles: \[ F_m'(y)=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_j}{(y-\eta_j)^2}<0. \] Each squared denominator is positive and each residue \(\rho_j\) is positive. Hence \(F_m\) is strictly decreasing on every such interval and has at most one zero there. Its derivative is nonzero at every zero, so each zero is simple.

Step 22: Locate all the zeros by their endpoint limits

Consider a gap \((\eta_j,\eta_{j+1})\). As \(y\to\eta_j^+\), \(\rho_j/(y-\eta_j)\to+\infty\); as \(y\to\eta_{j+1}^-\), \(\rho_{j+1}/(y-\eta_{j+1})\to-\infty\). The other terms have finite limits at the respective endpoints. Therefore \[ \lim_{y\to\eta_j^+}F_m(y)=+\infty,\qquad \lim_{y\to\eta_{j+1}^-}F_m(y)=-\infty. \] Continuity and strict decrease give exactly one zero in each gap.

On the leftmost interval, \[ \lim_{y\to-\infty}F_m(y)=d_m+s>0,\qquad \lim_{y\to\eta_1^-}F_m(y)=-\infty. \] There is exactly one zero there as well. On the rightmost interval \(y>\eta_m\), all the denominators \(y-\eta_j\) are positive, so \[ F_m(y)=d_m+s+\sum_j\frac{\rho_j}{y-\eta_j}>0. \] There are no zeros to the right of \(\eta_m\). We have therefore found exactly one simple zero in each of \[ (-\infty,\eta_1),\quad(\eta_1,\eta_2),\quad\ldots, \quad(\eta_{m-1},\eta_m), \] and hence \[ z_1<\eta_1<z_2<\eta_2<\cdots<z_m<\eta_m<0. \] These roots can therefore be characterized by real scalar bracketing without expanding the combined denominator into polynomial coefficients. Here real scalar bracketing means enclosing a zero of the real-valued function \(F_m\) between two real numbers \(a<b\) with \[ F_m(a)>0,\qquad F_m(b)<0. \] The function is continuous and strictly decreasing inside the corresponding source-pole interval, so these signs enclose exactly one zero. For example, bisection evaluates \(c=(a+b)/2\) and replaces \(a\) by \(c\) if \(F_m(c)>0\), or \(b\) by \(c\) if \(F_m(c)<0\); if \(F_m(c)=0\), the root has been found. Repeatedly narrowing the bracket gives the root to the desired tolerance. The source poles themselves are excluded because \(F_m\) is undefined there; their one-sided limits provide the endpoint signs.

For the leftmost root, a finite left endpoint is found by starting at \(2\eta_1\) and repeatedly doubling it until \(F_m\) is positive there. This search terminates because \(F_m(y)\to d_m+s>0\) as \(y\to-\infty\). The helper brasil_partial_fractions() maps each search interval to \(t\in[0,1]\), calls stats::uniroot(), and maps the result back to \(y\). At source-pole endpoints it supplies finite values with the signs of the one-sided infinite limits, so the function is not evaluated at a pole.

The polynomial \(p_r+s p_\ell\) has degree \(m\): its leading coefficient is \(c_0+s b_0=b_0(d_m+s)\ne0\). Since we have found \(m\) distinct real zeros, these exhaust its zeros and justify the expansion in \(\eqref{eq:partial_fraction}\).

Step 23: Derive the time-step residues

By the definition of a residue at a simple pole, \[ a_n=\lim_{y\to z_n}(y-z_n)H_m(y) =\lim_{y\to z_n} \frac{(y-z_n)S_m(y)}{F_m(y)}. \] Since \(F_m(z_n)=0\) and \[ \lim_{y\to z_n}\frac{F_m(y)}{y-z_n}=F_m'(z_n)=S_m'(z_n), \] the limit is \(S_m(z_n)/S_m'(z_n)\). Substituting \(S_m(z_n)=-s\) and \(S_m'(z_n)=-\sum_j\rho_j/(z_n-\eta_j)^2\) gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:residues} \tag{15} a_n=\lim_{y\to z_n}(y-z_n)H_m(y) =\frac{S_m(z_n)}{S_m'(z_n)} =\frac{s}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m} \rho_j/(z_n-\eta_j)^2}>0. \end{equation}\] The numerator \(s\) is positive, and the denominator is a sum of positive terms, proving \(a_n>0\).

Step 24: Prove positive definiteness of the shifted matrices

Using \(\mathbf L=\kappa^2\mathbf C+\mathbf G\), write \[ \mathbf M_n=\mathbf L/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf C =(1-z_n)\mathbf C+\mathbf G/\kappa^2. \] For any nonzero real vector \(\mathbf v\), \[ \mathbf v^\top\mathbf M_n\mathbf v =(1-z_n)\mathbf v^\top\mathbf C\mathbf v +\kappa^{-2}\mathbf v^\top\mathbf G\mathbf v>0. \] The strict inequality follows because \(z_n<0\), \(\mathbf C\) is positive definite, and \(\mathbf G\) is positive semidefinite. Thus every shifted matrix is real symmetric positive definite. The mass matrix is also positive definite, so all the solves in \(\eqref{eq:final_scheme3}\) admit sparse Cholesky factorizations, reusable at subsequent time steps.

Step 25: Check the decay of each unforced eigenmode

For \(y\ge1\), every denominator \(y-\eta_j\) is positive. Consequently \[ S_m(y)=d_m+\sum_j\frac{\rho_j}{y-\eta_j}>0,\qquad 0<H_m(y)=\frac{S_m(y)}{S_m(y)+s}<1. \] With zero forcing, the rational scheme is \[ \mathbf U_m^{k+1}=H_m(\mathbf A)\mathbf U_m^k. \] If \(\mathbf A\mathbf v_j=y_j\mathbf v_j\), where \(y_j\ge1\), then \(H_m(\mathbf A)\mathbf v_j=H_m(y_j)\mathbf v_j\). Each modal coefficient is multiplied at each step by a positive number strictly smaller than one. Every unforced eigenmode therefore decays without changing the sign of its coefficient.

The theoretical coefficient signs above follow from the best-approximation theorem. In the implementation, coefficients are stored numerically and may be interpolated in \(\beta\). The code checks the signs, distinctness, and finiteness of these numerical coefficients and verifies reconstruction of the time-step partial fractions. These are numerical validation checks; interpolated coefficients need not give the exact minimax approximation at the requested exponent. The numerical check accepts a nonnegative stored constant, whereas the exact theorem gives \(d_m>0\).

Integer power

Step 26: Recover ordinary backward Euler when the power is integer

For \(\alpha=2\), we have \(\beta=1\) and use the exact inverse \(S(y)=y^{-1}\). Then \[ H(y)=\frac{1}{1+\tau\nu\kappa^2y} =\frac{1}{\tau\nu\kappa^2} \left(y+\frac{1}{\tau\nu\kappa^2}\right)^{-1}. \] Here the single pole is \(-1/(\tau\nu\kappa^2)\), its residue is \(1/(\tau\nu\kappa^2)\), and the remainder is zero. The normalized scheme is \[ (\mathbf I+\tau\nu\kappa^2\mathbf A)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf F^{k+1}. \] Multiplying by \(\mathbf C\) and using \(\kappa^2\mathbf C\mathbf A=\mathbf L\) yields ordinary backward Euler, \[ (\mathbf C+\tau\nu\mathbf L)\mathbf U^{k+1} =\mathbf C\mathbf U^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}. \]

Step 27: Locate the source-coefficient table

The installed rSPDE namespace contains internal tables named m_brasil1t, m_brasil2t, and so on. Each table fixes the rational approximation order \(m\) and stores coefficients at a grid of exponents. The helper brasil_supported_orders() discovers the available orders from these names, so availability is determined by the installed package. For example, to inspect the order-three table:

brasil_supported_orders()
m <- 3L
tab <- get(paste0("m_brasil", m, "t"), envir = asNamespace("rSPDE"))
names(tab)

The table columns are interpreted as follows:

Table column Meaning in this derivation
alpha Approximation exponent \(\beta=\alpha_{\mathrm{PDE}}/2\)
p1, …, pm Source poles \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_m\) of \(S_m\)
r1, …, rm Corresponding source residues \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m\)
k Source constant \(d_m\)

The name alpha inside the table refers to the power being approximated; our PDE exponent must first be divided by two. These tables provide the partial fraction coefficients of \(S_m\) directly. We therefore do not need to construct \(q_1,q_2,p_r,p_\ell\) or compute their roots in order to use the solver.

Step 28: Select the exponent and extract paired coefficients

For a PDE exponent \(\alpha=1\), the approximation exponent is \(\beta=0.5\):

alpha <- 1
beta <- alpha / 2
coeff <- brasil_coefficients(m = m, beta = beta)

coeff$poles       # source poles eta_j
coeff$residues    # paired source residues rho_j
coeff$constant    # source constant d_m

The helper reads the table for the chosen \(m\) and looks for a row whose alpha matches \(\beta\) within a floating-point tolerance. If a matching row is found, it reads the values directly. Otherwise, it calls stats::approx() on each coefficient column to interpolate linearly between the neighboring tabulated exponents. In scalar notation, for \(\beta_a<\beta<\beta_b\) and any coefficient column \(g\), \[ g(\beta)\approx(1-\theta)g(\beta_a)+\theta g(\beta_b), \qquad \theta=\frac{\beta-\beta_a}{\beta_b-\beta_a}. \] The requested exponent must be covered by the table for interpolation to return finite values. The helper checks finiteness, negative distinct poles, positive residues, and a nonnegative numerical constant. It then sorts the poles increasingly and reorders the residues with the same permutation, preserving each pole-residue pair.

The table selection depends only on \(m\) and \(\beta\). Its source coefficients can be reused when \(h\), \(\tau\), \(\nu\), or \(\kappa\) changes. The mathematical sign properties of the exact best approximation are proved by Proposition 3; the checks here validate the numerical data being used, including any interpolated values.

Step 29: Form and evaluate the inverse-power approximation

With the extracted coefficients, evaluate \[ S_m(y)=d_m+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_j}{y-\eta_j}, \] using coeff$constant for \(d_m\), coeff$residues for the \(\rho_j\), and coeff$poles for the \(\eta_j\). The R helpers brasil_inverse(y, coeff) and brasil_power(x, coeff) evaluate \(S_m(y)\) and \(B_m(x)\), respectively:

brasil_inverse(c(1, 10, 100), coeff)
brasil_power(c(0, 0.1, 1), coeff)

For example, the installed order-three table at \(\beta=0.5\) gives, to the displayed precision, \[ S_3(y)\approx0.008386084 +\frac{25.09759}{y+400.8274} +\frac{3.573695}{y+13.41284} +\frac{0.9982498}{y+0.4681283}. \] This displays the source approximation before the diffusion coefficient or time step enters the calculation. The solver uses the full stored precision, rather than the rounded values displayed here.

Step 30: Convert source coefficients into time-step coefficients

Choose \(\tau\), \(\nu\), and \(\kappa\), and compute \[ s=\tau\nu\kappa^{2\beta}. \] For example, take \(\tau=0.01\), \(\nu=2\), and \(\kappa=4\), with \(\beta=0.5\) and \(m=3\) as above. Then \(s=0.08\):

tau <- 0.01
nu <- 2
kappa <- 4
transfer <- brasil_partial_fractions(
    coefficients = coeff,
    time_step = tau,
    scaling = nu * kappa^(2 * beta)
)

transfer$p       # time-step poles z_n
transfer$r       # time-step residues a_n
transfer$k       # time-step remainder r_tau

Inside this helper, strength = time_step * scaling is precisely \(s\). The function brackets and solves \[ d_m+s+\sum_j\frac{\rho_j}{z_n-\eta_j}=0 \] for each \(z_n\), and then evaluates \[ a_n=\frac{s}{\sum_j\rho_j/(z_n-\eta_j)^2},\qquad r_\tau=\frac{d_m}{d_m+s}. \] For this example, the computed values are approximately

\(n\) \(z_n\) \(a_n\)
1 \(-707.546917489\) \(289.654414217\)
2 \(-40.295926445\) \(13.871268382\)
3 \(-2.546597602\) \(0.305928594\)

with \(r_\tau\approx0.09488013631\). The source constant coeff$constant is \(d_m\), while the returned constant transfer$k is \(r_\tau\). Likewise, coeff$poles contains \(\eta_j\), while transfer$p contains \(z_n\); the two sets of coefficients describe different rational functions.

Finally, the helper compares \[ \frac{S_m(y)}{S_m(y)+s} \quad\text{and}\quad r_\tau+\sum_n\frac{a_n}{y-z_n} \] on a logarithmic grid of 257 points from \(1\) to \(10^{12}\) and rejects a reconstruction discrepancy greater than \(2\times10^{-12}\). This validates agreement between two representations of \(H_m\); it does not measure the approximation error between \(S_m(y)\) and \(y^{-\beta}\). Changing \(\tau\), \(\nu\), or \(\kappa\) changes \(s\), so the time-step coefficients are recomputed for the new strength.

Step 31: Build the shifted matrices and advance one time step

The operator-building function performs the preceding coefficient steps and constructs the matrices in \(\eqref{eq:final_scheme3}\):

op <- my.fractional.operators.frac(
    L = L, beta = beta, C = C,
    scale.factor = kappa^2,
    m = m, time_step = tau, diffusion = nu
)

rhs <- C %*% U_k + tau * F_next
U_next <- my.solver.frac(op, rhs)

Here L and C are the assembled matrices \(\mathbf L\) and \(\mathbf C\), U_k is \(\mathbf U_m^k\), and F_next is \(\mathbf F^{k+1}\). The argument diffusion is \(\nu\). The builder divides its input L by scale.factor, so each stored shifted matrix is \(\mathbf L/\kappa^2-z_n\mathbf C\), and its scalar scaling is \(\nu(\kappa^2)^\beta=\nu\kappa^{2\beta}\). The solver combines a mass-matrix solve with the shifted solves, weighted by \(r_\tau\) and \(a_n\), to produce \(\mathbf U_m^{k+1}\). The forcing is multiplied by \(\tau\) once in rhs; \(\nu\) enters through the time-step coefficients. With fixed parameters the operator object and its Cholesky factorizations are reused at every time step. For \(\beta=1\), the builder uses the exact matrix \(\mathbf C+\tau\nu\mathbf L\) directly and does not read a BRASIL table.

brasil_supported_orders <- function() {
    tables <- grep("^m_brasil[0-9]+t$", ls(asNamespace("rSPDE")), value = TRUE)
    sort(as.integer(sub("^m_brasil([0-9]+)t$", "\\1", tables)))
}

brasil_coefficients <- function(m, beta) {
    stopifnot(length(m) == 1L, is.finite(m), m %in% brasil_supported_orders(), length(beta) == 1L, is.finite(beta),
        beta > 0, beta < 1)
    table <- get(paste0("m_brasil", m, "t"), envir = asNamespace("rSPDE"))
    tolerance <- 32 * .Machine$double.eps * max(1, abs(beta))
    index <- which(abs(table$alpha - beta) <= tolerance)
    value <- function(column) {
        if (length(index) == 1L)
            table[[column]][index]
        else stats::approx(table$alpha, table[[column]], xout = beta)$y
    }
    poles <- vapply(paste0("p", seq_len(m)), value, numeric(1))
    residues <- vapply(paste0("r", seq_len(m)), value, numeric(1))
    constant <- value("k")
    if (any(!is.finite(c(poles, residues, constant))) || any(poles >= 0) || any(residues <= 0) || constant < 0 ||
        anyDuplicated(poles))
        stop("BRASIL table at m = ", m, ", beta = ", beta, " requires distinct negative poles, positive residues, and a nonnegative constant.")
    index <- order(poles)
    list(poles = unname(poles[index]), residues = unname(residues[index]), constant = unname(constant), m = as.integer(m),
        beta = beta)
}

brasil_inverse <- function(y, coefficients) {
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(y)), all(y > 0))
    coefficients$constant + as.numeric((1/outer(y, coefficients$poles, "-")) %*% coefficients$residues)
}

brasil_power <- function(x, coefficients) {
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(x)), all(x >= 0))
    coefficients$constant + as.numeric((x/(1 - outer(x, coefficients$poles))) %*% coefficients$residues)
}

brasil_partial_fractions <- function(coefficients, time_step, scaling) {
    strength <- time_step * scaling
    stopifnot(length(strength) == 1L, is.finite(strength), strength > 0)
    p <- coefficients$poles
    r <- coefficients$residues
    constant <- coefficients$constant
    poles <- numeric(length(p))
    denominator <- function(y) constant + strength + sum(r/(y - p))
    for (i in seq_along(p)) {
        upper <- p[i]
        lower <- if (i == 1L)
            2 * upper
        else p[i - 1L]
        if (i == 1L)
            while (denominator(lower) <= 0) lower <- 2 * lower
        width <- upper - lower
        normalized <- function(t) {
            if (t == 0 && i > 1L)
                return(.Machine$double.xmax)
            if (t == 1)
                return(-.Machine$double.xmax)
            denominator(lower + width * t)
        }
        root <- stats::uniroot(normalized, c(0, 1), tol = 2 * .Machine$double.eps, maxiter = 1000L)$root
        poles[i] <- lower + width * root
    }
    residues <- vapply(poles, function(z) strength/sum(r/(z - p)^2), numeric(1))
    remainder <- constant/(constant + strength)
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(c(poles, residues, remainder))), all(poles < 0), all(residues > 0), remainder >= 0,
        remainder < 1)
    y <- 10^seq(0, 12, length.out = 257L)
    inverse <- brasil_inverse(y, coefficients)
    expected <- inverse/(inverse + strength)
    actual <- remainder + as.numeric((1/outer(y, poles, "-")) %*% residues)
    reconstruction_error <- max(abs(actual - expected))
    if (reconstruction_error > 2e-12)
        stop("Inaccurate BRASIL time-step partial fractions: ", reconstruction_error)
    list(p = poles, r = residues, k = remainder, reconstruction_error = reconstruction_error)
}

my.fractional.operators.frac <- function(L, beta, C, scale.factor, m = 1, time_step, diffusion = 1) {
    stopifnot(length(diffusion) == 1L, is.finite(diffusion), diffusion > 0, length(time_step) == 1L, is.finite(time_step),
        time_step > 0, length(beta) == 1L, is.finite(beta), beta > 0, beta <= 1, length(scale.factor) == 1L, is.finite(scale.factor),
        scale.factor > 0)
    L <- L/scale.factor
    if (beta == 1) {
        L <- L * scale.factor^beta
        lhs <- C + time_step * diffusion * L
        return(list(C = C, L = L, m = m, beta = beta, diffusion = diffusion, LHS = lhs, factorization = Matrix::Cholesky(Matrix::forceSymmetric(lhs),
            LDL = FALSE)))
    }
    scaling <- diffusion * scale.factor^beta
    coefficients <- brasil_coefficients(m, beta)
    poles_rs_k <- brasil_partial_fractions(coefficients, time_step, scaling)
    partial_fraction_terms <- lapply(poles_rs_k$p, function(p) L - p * C)
    remainder <- if (length(poles_rs_k$k))
        poles_rs_k$k
    else 0
    list(C = C, L = L, m = m, beta = beta, diffusion = diffusion, partial_fraction_terms = partial_fraction_terms,
        residues = poles_rs_k$r, poles = poles_rs_k$p, remainder = remainder, mass_factor = if (remainder != 0) Matrix::Cholesky(Matrix::forceSymmetric(C),
            LDL = FALSE) else NULL, factorizations = lapply(partial_fraction_terms, function(A) Matrix::Cholesky(Matrix::forceSymmetric(A),
            LDL = FALSE)))
}

my.solver.frac <- function(obj, v) {
    if (obj$beta == 1) {
        return(if (!is.null(obj$factorization)) Matrix::solve(obj$factorization, v, system = "A") else Matrix::solve(obj$LHS, v))
    }
    partial_fraction_terms <- obj$partial_fraction_terms
    residues <- obj$residues
    output <- v * 0
    if (!is.null(obj$remainder) && obj$remainder != 0) {
        mass_solved <- if (!is.null(obj$mass_factor))
            Matrix::solve(obj$mass_factor, v, system = "A")
        else Matrix::solve(obj$C, v)
        output <- obj$remainder * mass_solved
    }
    for (i in seq_along(residues)) {
        solved <- if (!is.null(obj$factorizations)) {
            Matrix::solve(obj$factorizations[[i]], v, system = "A")
        }
        else Matrix::solve(partial_fraction_terms[[i]], v)
        output <- output + residues[i] * solved
    }
    return(output)
}

prepare_reference_error <- function(Psi, reference_C, reference_basis) {
    E <- as.matrix(reference_basis)
    stopifnot(nrow(Psi) == nrow(reference_C), nrow(reference_C) == ncol(reference_C), nrow(E) == nrow(Psi), ncol(E) >=
        1L, all(is.finite(E)))
    metric <- Matrix::forceSymmetric(as(Matrix::Matrix(Matrix::crossprod(Psi, reference_C %*% Psi), sparse = TRUE),
        "CsparseMatrix"))
    cross <- Matrix::crossprod(Psi, reference_C %*% E)
    factor <- Matrix::Cholesky(metric, LDL = FALSE)
    projection <- as.matrix(Matrix::solve(factor, cross, system = "A"))
    residual <- E - as.matrix(Psi %*% projection)
    list(metric = metric, projection = projection, residual_gram = as.matrix(Matrix::crossprod(residual, reference_C %*%
        residual)), exact_gram = as.matrix(Matrix::crossprod(E, reference_C %*% E)))
}

reference_coefficient_moments <- function(coefficients, weights) {
    coefficients <- as.matrix(coefficients)
    stopifnot(nrow(coefficients) >= 1L, ncol(coefficients) == length(weights), length(weights) >= 1L, all(is.finite(coefficients)),
        all(is.finite(weights)), all(weights > 0))
    weight <- sum(weights)
    centered <- sweep(coefficients, 1L, coefficients[, 1L], `-`)
    shift <- as.numeric(centered %*% (weights/weight))
    deviations <- sweep(centered, 1L, shift, `-`)
    list(weight = weight, mean = coefficients[, 1L] + shift, scatter = tcrossprod(sweep(deviations, 2L, sqrt(weights),
        `*`)))
}

reference_interval_error <- function(u, moments, projection) {
    delta <- as.numeric(u - projection$projection %*% moments$mean)
    metric_product <- as.numeric(projection$metric %*% delta)
    residual_product <- as.numeric(projection$residual_gram %*% moments$mean)
    variance_terms <- projection$exact_gram * moments$scatter
    value <- moments$weight * (sum(delta * metric_product) + sum(moments$mean * residual_product)) + sum(variance_terms)
    scale <- moments$weight * (sum(abs(delta * metric_product)) + sum(abs(moments$mean * residual_product))) +
        sum(abs(variance_terms))
    if (!is.finite(value) || value < -128 * .Machine$double.eps * scale)
        stop("Invalid reference-grid squared error.")
    max(0, value)
}

# R reference retained for numerical regression checks.
solve_fractional_evolution_error_R <- function(my_op_frac, time_step, time_seq, val_at_0, spatial_load, g_average,
    reference_times, coefficients_at_time, error_projection, verbose = FALSE) {
    stopifnot(length(time_step) == 1L, is.finite(time_step), time_step > 0,
        length(time_seq) >= 2L, length(reference_times) >= 2L,
        all(is.finite(time_seq)), all(is.finite(reference_times)),
        all(diff(time_seq) > 0), all(diff(reference_times) > 0),
        reference_times[1] == time_seq[1], tail(reference_times, 1) == tail(time_seq, 1),
        length(g_average) == length(time_seq), length(val_at_0) == nrow(my_op_frac$C),
        length(spatial_load) == length(val_at_0), is.function(coefficients_at_time),
        is.logical(verbose), length(verbose) == 1L, !is.na(verbose),
        nrow(error_projection$metric) == length(val_at_0),
        ncol(error_projection$metric) == length(val_at_0),
        nrow(error_projection$projection) == length(val_at_0))
    n_modes <- ncol(error_projection$projection)
    stopifnot(identical(dim(error_projection$exact_gram), c(n_modes, n_modes)),
        identical(dim(error_projection$residual_gram), c(n_modes, n_modes)))
    times <- reference_times[-1L]
    weights <- diff(reference_times)
    coefficients <- as.matrix(coefficients_at_time(times))
    stopifnot(nrow(coefficients) == n_modes, ncol(coefficients) == length(times),
        all(is.finite(coefficients)))
    runs <- rle(findInterval(times, time_seq, left.open = TRUE))
    u <- as.numeric(val_at_0)
    spatial_load <- as.numeric(spatial_load)
    solver_step <- 0L
    first_column <- 1L
    total <- compensation <- 0
    started <- proc.time()[["elapsed"]]
    for (run in seq_along(runs$values)) {
        target <- runs$values[run]
        while (solver_step < target) {
            solver_step <- solver_step + 1L
            rhs <- my_op_frac$C %*% u + time_step * spatial_load * g_average[solver_step + 1L]
            u <- as.numeric(my.solver.frac(my_op_frac, rhs))
        }
        last_column <- first_column + runs$lengths[run] - 1L
        columns <- seq.int(first_column, last_column)
        moments <- reference_coefficient_moments(coefficients[, columns, drop = FALSE], weights[columns])
        value <- reference_interval_error(u, moments, error_projection)
        adjusted <- value - compensation
        updated <- total + adjusted
        compensation <- (updated - total) - adjusted
        total <- updated
        first_column <- last_column + 1L
    }
    stopifnot(first_column == length(times) + 1L, solver_step == length(time_seq) - 1L)
    if (verbose)
        message(sprintf("Reference error: %s time samples, %s solver steps, %.2f s elapsed",
            length(times), solver_step, proc.time()[["elapsed"]] - started))
    sqrt(total)
}

# Compile once in the parent session; forked workers inherit the loaded kernel.
smooth_compile_solver <- function(cache_dir=tempdir()) {
    target <- environment(smooth_compile_solver)
    if (!exists("smooth_reference_evolve_cpp", envir=target, inherits=FALSE))
        Rcpp::sourceCpp(code=r"----(
// [[Rcpp::depends(RcppEigen)]]
// [[Rcpp::plugins(cpp11)]]
#include <RcppEigen.h>
#include <memory>
#include <vector>
#include <cmath>
#include <limits>
using Eigen::VectorXd;
using Eigen::MatrixXd;
using Eigen::SparseMatrix;
typedef Eigen::SimplicialLLT<SparseMatrix<double> > SmoothFactor;

// Backward Euler/BRASIL updates and the same centered sampled-error identity
// as the R reference implementation. Every solver step is advanced.
// [[Rcpp::export]]
Rcpp::List smooth_reference_evolve_cpp(
    const Eigen::MappedSparseMatrix<double>& C_in,
    Rcpp::List matrices, VectorXd residues, double remainder,
    VectorXd initial, VectorXd load, VectorXd forcing,
    MatrixXd projection,
    const Eigen::MappedSparseMatrix<double>& metric_in,
    MatrixXd residual_gram, MatrixXd exact_gram, MatrixXd coefficients,
    Rcpp::IntegerVector target_steps, VectorXd weights,
    double tau, int n_steps) {
    Eigen::setNbThreads(1);
    SparseMatrix<double> C=C_in, metric=metric_in;
    const int n=C.rows(), modes=projection.cols();
    const int samples=target_steps.size(), m=residues.size();
    if (n_steps<1 || !std::isfinite(tau) || tau<=0 || samples<1 ||
        C.cols()!=n || matrices.size()!=m || initial.size()!=n || load.size()!=n ||
        forcing.size()!=n_steps+1 || projection.rows()!=n || modes<1 ||
        coefficients.rows()!=modes || coefficients.cols()!=samples ||
        weights.size()!=samples || metric.rows()!=n || metric.cols()!=n ||
        residual_gram.rows()!=modes || residual_gram.cols()!=modes ||
        exact_gram.rows()!=modes || exact_gram.cols()!=modes)
        Rcpp::stop("Invalid smooth-reference dimensions or time grid.");
    SmoothFactor mass_factor;
    if (remainder!=0) {
        mass_factor.compute(C);
        if (mass_factor.info()!=Eigen::Success)
            Rcpp::stop("Mass factorization failed.");
    }
    std::vector<std::unique_ptr<SmoothFactor> > factors;
    for (int i=0;i<m;++i) {
        SparseMatrix<double> A=Rcpp::as<SparseMatrix<double> >(matrices[i]);
        if (A.rows()!=n || A.cols()!=n)
            Rcpp::stop("Invalid shifted matrix dimensions.");
        factors.emplace_back(new SmoothFactor());
        factors.back()->compute(A);
        if (factors.back()->info()!=Eigen::Success)
            Rcpp::stop("Shifted factorization failed.");
    }
    VectorXd u=initial, rhs(n), next(n), delta(n), metric_product(n);
    VectorXd anchor(modes), shift(modes), mean(modes), centered(modes);
    VectorXd deviation(modes), residual_product(modes);
    MatrixXd scatter(modes,modes);
    int step=0, first=0;
    double total=0, compensation=0;
    while (first<samples) {
        Rcpp::checkUserInterrupt();
        const int goal=target_steps[first];
        if (goal<1 || goal<step || goal>n_steps)
            Rcpp::stop("Invalid reference time-to-step mapping.");
        while (step<goal) {
            if ((step & 4095)==0) Rcpp::checkUserInterrupt();
            ++step;
            rhs.noalias()=C*u;
            rhs.noalias()+=tau*load*forcing[step];
            if (remainder!=0) next=remainder*mass_factor.solve(rhs);
            else next.setZero();
            for (int i=0;i<m;++i)
                next.noalias()+=residues[i]*factors[i]->solve(rhs);
            u.swap(next);
        }
        int last=first+1;
        while (last<samples && target_steps[last]==goal) ++last;
        long double weight_sum=0;
        for (int q=first;q<last;++q) {
            if (!std::isfinite(weights[q]) || weights[q]<=0)
                Rcpp::stop("Reference weights must be positive and finite.");
            weight_sum+=weights[q];
        }
        const double weight=static_cast<double>(weight_sum);
        anchor=coefficients.col(first);
        shift.setZero();
        for (int q=first;q<last;++q) {
            if ((q & 4095)==0) Rcpp::checkUserInterrupt();
            centered=coefficients.col(q)-anchor;
            shift.noalias()+=(weights[q]/weight)*centered;
        }
        mean=anchor+shift;
        scatter.setZero();
        for (int q=first;q<last;++q) {
            centered=coefficients.col(q)-anchor;
            deviation=std::sqrt(weights[q])*(centered-shift);
            scatter.noalias()+=deviation*deviation.transpose();
        }
        delta.noalias()=u-projection*mean;
        metric_product.noalias()=metric*delta;
        residual_product.noalias()=residual_gram*mean;
        const double variance=exact_gram.cwiseProduct(scatter).sum();
        double value=weight*(delta.dot(metric_product)+mean.dot(residual_product))+variance;
        const double scale=weight*(delta.cwiseProduct(metric_product).cwiseAbs().sum()+
            mean.cwiseProduct(residual_product).cwiseAbs().sum())+
            exact_gram.cwiseProduct(scatter).cwiseAbs().sum();
        if (!std::isfinite(value) || value < -128*std::numeric_limits<double>::epsilon()*scale)
            Rcpp::stop("Invalid reference-grid squared error.");
        value=std::max(0.0,value);
        const double adjusted=value-compensation;
        const double updated=total+adjusted;
        compensation=(updated-total)-adjusted;
        total=updated;
        first=last;
    }
    if (step!=n_steps) Rcpp::stop("Reference grid did not reach the final solver step.");
    return Rcpp::List::create(Rcpp::Named("error")=std::sqrt(total),
        Rcpp::Named("final_state")=u, Rcpp::Named("solver_steps")=step);
}
)----", env=target, cacheDir=cache_dir, showOutput=FALSE, verbose=FALSE)
    invisible(TRUE)
}

average_sine_forcing <- function(time_seq, A = 1, omega = pi) {
    dt <- diff(time_seq)
    stopifnot(length(time_seq) >= 2L, all(is.finite(time_seq)), all(dt > 0))
    midpoint <- head(time_seq, -1) + dt/2
    z <- omega * dt/2
    sinc_z <- rep(1, length(z))

    sinc_z[nonzero] <- sin(z[nonzero])/z[nonzero]
    averages <- A * sin(omega * midpoint) * sinc_z
    c(0, averages)
}

tadpole.eig <- function(k, graph) {
    x1 <- c(0, graph$get_edge_lengths()[1] * graph$mesh$PtE[graph$mesh$PtE[, 1] == 1, 2])
    x2 <- c(0, graph$get_edge_lengths()[2] * graph$mesh$PtE[graph$mesh$PtE[, 1] == 2, 2])
    if (k == 0) {
        f.e1 <- rep(1, length(x1))
        f.e2 <- rep(1, length(x2))
        f1 = c(f.e1[1], f.e2[1], f.e1[-1], f.e2[-1])
        f = list(phi = f1/sqrt(3))
    }
    else {
        f.e1 <- -2 * sin(pi * k * 1/2) * cos(pi * k * x1/2)
        f.e2 <- sin(pi * k * x2/2)
        f1 = c(f.e1[1], f.e2[1], f.e1[-1], f.e2[-1])
        if ((k%%2) == 1) {
            f = list(phi = f1/sqrt(3))
        }
        else {
            f.e1 <- (-1)^{
                k/2
            } * cos(pi * k * x1/2)
            f.e2 <- cos(pi * k * x2/2)
            f2 = c(f.e1[1], f.e2[1], f.e1[-1], f.e2[-1])
            f <- list(phi = f1, psi = f2/sqrt(3/2))
        }
    }
    return(f)
}

gets.eigen.params <- function(N_finite = 4, kappa = 1, alpha = 0.5, graph) {
    EIGENVAL <- NULL
    EIGENVAL_ALPHA <- NULL
    EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA <- NULL
    EIGENFUN <- NULL
    INDEX <- NULL
    for (j in 0:N_finite) {
        lambda_j <- kappa^2 + (j * pi/2)^2
        lambda_j_alpha_half <- lambda_j^(alpha/2)
        lambda_j_minus_alpha_half <- lambda_j^(-alpha/2)
        e_j <- tadpole.eig(j, graph)$phi
        EIGENVAL <- c(EIGENVAL, lambda_j)
        EIGENVAL_ALPHA <- c(EIGENVAL_ALPHA, lambda_j_alpha_half)
        EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA <- c(EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA, lambda_j_minus_alpha_half)
        EIGENFUN <- cbind(EIGENFUN, e_j)
        INDEX <- c(INDEX, j)
        if (j > 0 && (j%%2 == 0)) {
            lambda_j <- kappa^2 + (j * pi/2)^2
            lambda_j_alpha_half <- lambda_j^(alpha/2)
            lambda_j_minus_alpha_half <- lambda_j^(-alpha/2)
            e_j <- tadpole.eig(j, graph)$psi
            EIGENVAL <- c(EIGENVAL, lambda_j)
            EIGENVAL_ALPHA <- c(EIGENVAL_ALPHA, lambda_j_alpha_half)
            EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA <- c(EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA, lambda_j_minus_alpha_half)
            EIGENFUN <- cbind(EIGENFUN, e_j)
            INDEX <- c(INDEX, j + 0.1)
        }
    }
    return(list(EIGENVAL = EIGENVAL, EIGENVAL_ALPHA = EIGENVAL_ALPHA, EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA = EIGENVAL_MINUS_ALPHA,
        EIGENFUN = EIGENFUN, INDEX = INDEX))
}

trig_hat_integrals <- function(a, b, w, A = 0, B = 0) {
    h <- b - a
    z <- w * h/2
    theta <- w * (a + b)/2
    if (abs(z) < 0.001) {
        S <- 1 - z^2/6 + z^4/120 - z^6/5040
        Q <- z/3 - z^3/30 + z^5/840 - z^7/45360
    }
    else {
        S <- sin(z)/z
        Q <- (sin(z) - z * cos(z))/z^2
    }
    v <- A * cos(theta) + B * sin(theta)
    q <- A * sin(theta) - B * cos(theta)
    h/2 * c(left = S * v + Q * q, right = S * v - Q * q)
}

project_u0_tadpole <- function(graph, amplitude = 10) {
    C <- graph$mesh$C
    PtE <- graph$mesh$PtE
    lengths <- graph$get_edge_lengths()
    stopifnot(!is.null(C), length(lengths) == 2L, nrow(C) == 2L + nrow(PtE), length(amplitude) == 1L, is.finite(amplitude))
    local_load <- function(a, b, w, A, B) {
        h <- b - a
        z <- w * h/2
        theta <- w * (a + b)/2
        if (abs(z) < 0.001) {
            S <- 1 - z^2/6 + z^4/120 - z^6/5040
            Q <- z/3 - z^3/30 + z^5/840 - z^7/45360
        }
        else {
            S <- sin(z)/z
            Q <- (sin(z) - z * cos(z))/z^2
        }
        v <- A * cos(theta) + B * sin(theta)
        q <- A * sin(theta) - B * cos(theta)
        h/2 * c(S * v + Q * q, S * v - Q * q)
    }
    b0 <- numeric(nrow(C))
    w <- 3 * pi/2
    for (edge in 1:2) {
        idx <- which(PtE[, 1] == edge)
        idx <- idx[order(PtE[idx, 2])]
        x <- c(0, lengths[edge] * PtE[idx, 2], lengths[edge])
        start_node <- if (edge == 1L)
            1L
        else 2L
        nodes <- c(start_node, 2L + idx, 2L)
        A <- if (edge == 1L)
            2 * amplitude/sqrt(3)
        else 0
        B <- if (edge == 2L)
            amplitude/sqrt(3)
        else 0
        for (j in seq_len(length(x) - 1L)) {
            load <- local_load(x[j], x[j + 1L], w, A, B)
            b0[nodes[j]] <- b0[nodes[j]] + load[1]
            b0[nodes[j + 1L]] <- b0[nodes[j + 1L]] + load[2]
        }
    }
    as.numeric(Matrix::solve(C, b0))
}

decayed_sine_convolution <- function(t, rate, A = 1, omega = pi) {
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(t)), all(t >= 0), length(rate) == 1L, is.finite(rate), rate >= 0, is.finite(A), is.finite(omega))
    z <- (rate + (0+1i) * omega) * t
    small <- Mod(z) < 0.5
    answer <- numeric(length(t))
    if (any(small)) {
        zz <- z[small]
        term <- rep(1 + (0+0i), length(zz))
        series <- rep(0 + (0+0i), length(zz))
        for (k in 1:24) {
            term <- term * zz/(k + 1)
            series <- series + term
        }
        answer[small] <- A * exp(-rate * t[small]) * t[small] * Im(series)
    }
    if (any(!small)) {
        tt <- t[!small]
        answer[!small] <- A * Im((exp((0+1i) * omega * tt) - exp(-rate * tt))/(rate + (0+1i) * omega))
    }
    answer
}

exact_modal_coefficients <- function(t, rates, initial, forcing, A = 1, omega = pi) {
    stopifnot(length(rates) == length(initial), length(rates) == length(forcing), all(is.finite(rates)), all(rates >
        0))
    out <- matrix(0, nrow = length(rates), ncol = length(t))
    for (j in seq_along(rates)) {
        out[j, ] <- initial[j] * exp(-rates[j] * t) + forcing[j] * decayed_sine_convolution(t, rates[j], A = A,
            omega = omega)
    }
    out
}

run_reference_cases <- function(cases, FUN, workers = 1L) {
    stopifnot(is.function(FUN), length(workers) == 1L, is.finite(workers), workers >= 1L, workers == as.integer(workers))
    if (!length(cases))
        return(list())
    workers <- min(as.integer(workers), length(cases))
    task <- function(x) tryCatch(list(value = FUN(x)), error = function(e) list(error = conditionMessage(e)))
    if (workers > 1L) {
        if (.Platform$OS.type != "unix")
            stop("Parallel reference cases require Unix fork workers; set workers = 1 on this platform.")
        results <- parallel::mclapply(cases, task, mc.cores = workers, mc.preschedule = FALSE)
    }
    else results <- lapply(cases, task)
    failed <- which(vapply(results, function(x) inherits(x, "try-error") || !is.list(x) || !is.null(x$error) ||
        !("value" %in% names(x)), logical(1)))
    if (length(failed)) {
        i <- failed[1L]
        detail <- if (is.list(results[[i]]))
            results[[i]]$error
        else as.character(results[[i]])
        stop("Reference case ", i, " failed: ", paste(detail, collapse = " "), call. = FALSE)
    }
    lapply(results, `[[`, "value")
}

Discrete references and error diagnostics

Backward Euler reference in the eigenvector basis

For the isolated studies in rational order, the reference uses the fractional power of the assembled FEM operator. Set \[ \mathbf B=\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf L,\qquad \beta=\alpha/2. \] Its backward Euler equation is \[ \mathbf C\frac{\mathbf U_h^{k+1}-\mathbf U_h^k}{\tau} +\nu\mathbf C\mathbf B^\beta\mathbf U_h^{k+1} =\mathbf F^{k+1}, \] where \(\mathbf F^{k+1}\) is the load vector for the interval-averaged forcing. We compute the complete generalized eigensystem \[ \mathbf L\mathbf V=\mathbf C\mathbf V\boldsymbol\Lambda, \qquad \mathbf V^\top\mathbf C\mathbf V=\mathbf I, \qquad \boldsymbol\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{j,h}). \]

Write the solution in the eigenvector basis: \[ \mathbf U_h^k=\mathbf V\mathbf x^k. \] Multiplying by \(\mathbf V^\top\mathbf C\) recovers its coefficients: \[ \mathbf x^k=\mathbf V^\top\mathbf C\mathbf U_h^k. \] Also, the generalized eigenvalue equation gives \[ \mathbf B\mathbf V=\mathbf V\boldsymbol\Lambda \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf B^\beta\mathbf V=\mathbf V\boldsymbol\Lambda^\beta. \] Substitute \(\mathbf U_h^k=\mathbf V\mathbf x^k\) into backward Euler: \[ \mathbf C\mathbf V\frac{\mathbf x^{k+1}-\mathbf x^k}{\tau} +\nu\mathbf C\mathbf V\boldsymbol\Lambda^\beta\mathbf x^{k+1} =\mathbf F^{k+1}. \] Now multiply on the left by \(\mathbf V^\top\). Since \(\mathbf V^\top\mathbf C\mathbf V=\mathbf I\), this becomes \[ \frac{\mathbf x^{k+1}-\mathbf x^k}{\tau} +\nu\boldsymbol\Lambda^\beta\mathbf x^{k+1} =\mathbf V^\top\mathbf F^{k+1}. \] Rearranging gives \[ \mathbf x^{k+1} =(\mathbf I+\tau\nu\boldsymbol\Lambda^\beta)^{-1} \left(\mathbf x^k+\tau\mathbf V^\top\mathbf F^{k+1}\right). \] The inverse is diagonal, so each coefficient is updated independently: \[ x_j^{k+1} =\frac{x_j^k+\tau(\mathbf V^\top\mathbf F^{k+1})_j} {1+\tau\nu\lambda_{j,h}^\beta}. \] Finally, substitute \(\mathbf x^k=\mathbf V^\top\mathbf C\mathbf U_h^k\) and reconstruct \(\mathbf U_h^{k+1}=\mathbf V\mathbf x^{k+1}\): \[ \boxed{ \mathbf U_h^{k+1} =\mathbf V(\mathbf I+\tau\nu\boldsymbol\Lambda^\beta)^{-1} \mathbf V^\top(\mathbf C\mathbf U_h^k+\tau\mathbf F^{k+1}). } \]

In isolated_exact_be_reference() below, rates contains \(\nu\lambda_{j,h}^\beta\), mu contains \((1+\tau\nu\lambda_{j,h}^\beta)^{-1}\), and y contains the current modal coefficients. For the separable load, f * g[k + 1L] is \(\mathbf V^\top\mathbf F^{k+1}\). The update y <- mu * (y + dt * f * g[k + 1L]) therefore implements the scalar formula for every mode, and V %*% y reconstructs the FEM coefficients. The rough reference uses the same update with constant forcing and zero initial data. Every numerical eigenmode is retained. Here “exact” refers to the discrete fractional power, up to eigendecomposition and arithmetic errors; the reference still uses backward Euler in time.

isolated_discrete_spectrum <- function(C, L, max_dimension = 1200L, max_dense_mib = 1024, residual_tolerance = 1e-08,
    orthogonality_tolerance = 1e-10) {
    n <- nrow(C)
    stopifnot(n >= 1L, ncol(C) == n, identical(dim(L), dim(C)), Matrix::isSymmetric(C, tol = 1e-12), Matrix::isSymmetric(L, tol = 1e-12),
        length(max_dimension) == 1L, is.finite(max_dimension), max_dimension >= 1, length(max_dense_mib) == 1L,
        is.finite(max_dense_mib), max_dense_mib > 0, is.finite(residual_tolerance), residual_tolerance > 0, is.finite(orthogonality_tolerance),
        orthogonality_tolerance > 0)
    estimated_mib <- 20 * 8 * as.double(n)^2/1024^2
    if (n > max_dimension || estimated_mib > max_dense_mib)
        stop("Complete eigendecomposition refused before dense allocation: dimension ", n, ", estimated temporary storage ",
            round(estimated_mib, 1), " MiB. Choose a moderate isolated-study mesh or explicitly raise the guards.")
    started <- proc.time()[["elapsed"]]
    dense_C <- as.matrix(C)
    dense_L <- as.matrix(L)
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(dense_C)), all(is.finite(dense_L)))
    R <- chol(dense_C)
    Rinv <- backsolve(R, diag(n))
    symmetric <- Matrix::crossprod(Rinv, dense_L %*% Rinv)
    ee <- eigen((symmetric + t(symmetric))/2, symmetric = TRUE)
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(ee$values)), all(ee$values > 0))
    V <- backsolve(R, ee$vectors)
    CV <- C %*% V
    LV <- L %*% V
    residual <- as.matrix(LV - sweep(as.matrix(CV), 2L, ee$values, `*`))
    denominator <- sqrt(colSums(as.matrix(LV)^2)) + abs(ee$values) * sqrt(colSums(as.matrix(CV)^2))
    residual_error <- max(sqrt(colSums(residual^2))/pmax(denominator, .Machine$double.xmin))
    orthogonality_error <- max(abs(Matrix::crossprod(V, CV) - diag(n)))
    if (residual_error > residual_tolerance || orthogonality_error > orthogonality_tolerance)
        stop("Numerical eigensystem failed validation: residual = ", residual_error, ", C-orthogonality = ", orthogonality_error)
    list(values = ee$values, vectors = V, diagnostics = data.frame(dimension = n, eigenvalue_min = min(ee$values),
        eigenvalue_max = max(ee$values), relative_residual = residual_error, C_orthogonality_error = orthogonality_error,
        estimated_dense_mib = estimated_mib, elapsed_seconds = proc.time()[["elapsed"]] - started))
}

isolated_exact_be_reference <- function(C, spectrum, initial, spatial_load, time_seq, alpha, diffusion = 1, A = 1,
    omega = pi, max_history_mib = 256) {
    n <- nrow(C)
    stopifnot(length(initial) == n, length(spatial_load) == n, all(is.finite(initial)), all(is.finite(spatial_load)),
        length(time_seq) >= 2L, all(is.finite(time_seq)), all(diff(time_seq) > 0), length(alpha) == 1L, is.finite(alpha),
        alpha > 0, alpha <= 2, length(diffusion) == 1L, is.finite(diffusion), diffusion > 0, length(A) == 1L, is.finite(A),
        length(omega) == 1L, is.finite(omega), is.finite(max_history_mib), max_history_mib > 0, identical(dim(spectrum$vectors),
            c(n, n)), length(spectrum$values) == n)
    dt <- diff(time_seq)[1L]
    stopifnot(max(abs(diff(time_seq) - dt)) <= 128 * .Machine$double.eps * max(1, abs(tail(time_seq, 1L))))
    history_mib <- 2 * 8 * as.double(n) * length(time_seq)/1024^2
    if (history_mib > max_history_mib)
        stop("Exact reference history exceeds max_history_mib; use a moderate time grid.")
    V <- spectrum$vectors
    x <- as.numeric(Matrix::crossprod(V, C %*% initial))
    f <- as.numeric(Matrix::crossprod(V, spatial_load))
    rates <- diffusion * spectrum$values^(alpha/2)
    mu <- 1/(1 + dt * rates)
    g <- average_sine_forcing(time_seq, A = A, omega = omega)
    states <- coefficients <- matrix(0, n, length(time_seq))
    states[, 1L] <- initial
    coefficients[, 1L] <- x
    y <- x
    for (k in seq_len(length(time_seq) - 1L)) {
        y <- mu * (y + dt * f * g[k + 1L])
        coefficients[, k + 1L] <- y
        states[, k + 1L] <- as.numeric(V %*% y)
    }
    list(states = states, coefficients = coefficients, initial_coefficients = x, forcing_coefficients = f, exact_rates = rates,
        g_average = g, dt = dt, time_seq = time_seq, alpha = alpha, diffusion = diffusion, history_mib = history_mib)
}

    for (edge in 1:2) {

        nodes <- c(if (edge == 1L) 1L else 2L, 2L + idx, 2L)
        element_lengths <- c(element_lengths, diff(coordinates))
        for (i in seq_len(length(coordinates) - 1L)) {
            pair <- trig_hat_integrals(coordinates[i], coordinates[i + 1L], 2 * pi, A = forcing_amplitude * sqrt(2/3))

    list(C = C, L = L, initial = project_u0_tadpole(graph, initial_amplitude), spatial_load = load, kappa = kappa,

isolated_rational_rates <- function(eigenvalues, alpha, m, kappa, diffusion=1, coefficients=NULL) {
  if(alpha==2) return(diffusion*eigenvalues)
  if(is.null(coefficients)) coefficients <- brasil_coefficients(m,alpha/2)
  diffusion*kappa^alpha/brasil_inverse(eigenvalues/kappa^2,coefficients)
}

isolated_beta_bracket <- function(m,beta) {
  table <- get(paste0("m_brasil",m,"t"),asNamespace("rSPDE"))
  grid <- table$alpha; tolerance <- 32*.Machine$double.eps*max(1,abs(beta))
  list(exact=any(abs(grid-beta)<=tolerance),lower=max(grid[grid<=beta+tolerance]),
       upper=min(grid[grid>=beta-tolerance]))
}


isolated_rational_error <- function(data,spectrum,reference,m,excitation_tolerance=1e-8) {
    started <- proc.time()[["elapsed"]]
    stopifnot(nrow(reference$states) == nrow(data$C), ncol(reference$states) == length(reference$time_seq))
    coefficients <- if(reference$alpha==2) NULL else brasil_coefficients(m,reference$alpha/2)
    op <- my.fractional.operators.frac(data$L,reference$alpha/2,data$C,data$kappa^2,
      m,reference$dt,reference$diffusion)
    rates <- isolated_rational_rates(spectrum$values,reference$alpha,m,data$kappa,
      reference$diffusion,coefficients)
    mu <- 1/(1 + reference$dt * rates)
    y <- reference$initial_coefficients
    u <- as.numeric(data$initial)
    total <- spectral_total <- compensation <- spectral_compensation <- 0
    for (k in seq_len(length(reference$time_seq) - 1L)) {
        forcing <- reference$g_average[k + 1L]
        rhs <- data$C %*% u + reference$dt * data$spatial_load * forcing
        u <- as.numeric(my.solver.frac(op, rhs))
        delta <- reference$states[, k + 1L] - u
        value <- reference$dt * sum(delta * (data$C %*% delta))
        adjusted <- value - compensation
        updated <- total + adjusted
        compensation <- (updated - total) - adjusted
        total <- updated
        y <- mu * (y + reference$dt * reference$forcing_coefficients * forcing)
        dy <- reference$coefficients[, k + 1L] - y
        value <- reference$dt * sum(dy^2)
        adjusted <- value - spectral_compensation
        updated <- spectral_total + adjusted
        spectral_compensation <- (updated - spectral_total) - adjusted
        spectral_total <- updated
    }
    sparse_error <- sqrt(max(0, total))
    product_error <- sqrt(max(0, spectral_total))
    gap <- u - as.numeric(spectrum$vectors %*% y)
    final_gap <- sqrt(max(0, sum(gap * (data$C %*% gap))))
    excited <- abs(reference$initial_coefficients) > excitation_tolerance * max(abs(reference$initial_coefficients)) |
        abs(reference$forcing_coefficients) > excitation_tolerance * max(abs(reference$forcing_coefficients))
    relative_rate_error <- rates/reference$exact_rates - 1
    bracket <- if (reference$alpha == 2)
        list(exact = TRUE, lower = 1, upper = 1)
    else isolated_beta_bracket(m,reference$alpha/2)
    data.frame(alpha = reference$alpha, diffusion = reference$diffusion, m = m, error = sparse_error, product_error = product_error,
        absolute_error_disagreement = abs(sparse_error - product_error), relative_error_disagreement = abs(sparse_error -
            product_error)/max(product_error, .Machine$double.xmin), final_state_gap_C = final_gap, max_relative_rate_error_full = max(abs(relative_rate_error)),
        max_relative_rate_error_excited = if (any(excited))
            max(abs(relative_rate_error[excited]))
        else 0, excited_numerical_modes = sum(excited), coefficient_source = "brasil", coefficient_beta_exact = bracket$exact,
        beta_exact_in_package_table = bracket$exact, beta_table_lower = bracket$lower, beta_table_upper = bracket$upper, elapsed_seconds = proc.time()[["elapsed"]] -
            started)
}

rough_rates <- function(j, alpha, diffusion = 1, kappa = 4) {
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(j)), all(j >= 1), all(j == floor(j)), length(alpha) == 1L, is.finite(alpha), alpha >
        0, alpha < 2, length(diffusion) == 1L, is.finite(diffusion), diffusion > 0, length(kappa) == 1L, is.finite(kappa),

}

rough_rational_coefficients <- function(beta, order = 16L) {

rough_tau_mesh <- function(J, kappa = 4) {

        kappa > 0)
    h <- 2^-J

        h/3, n - 1L)))

Sampled reference-mesh errors for rough forcing

The reference below is the nodal evaluation of a finite continuum series. Its consistent P1 mass matrix and right-endpoint time samples define the same sampled norm as the smooth reference experiment. Factor-four quarter-loop matrices preserve the full-graph norm for these data. The compiled kernel advances every shifted FEM matrix step and evaluates the sampled quadratic norm; no analytic time integral or continuum-tail term is added. Reference cutoff and mesh resolution are explicit inputs.

rough_sampled_reference <- function(J_reference, cutoff, max_nodes = 2^22,
                                    max_basis_mib = 2048) {
    stopifnot(length(J_reference)==1L,is.finite(J_reference),
      J_reference==as.integer(J_reference),J_reference>=4L,J_reference<=23L,
      length(cutoff)==1L,is.finite(cutoff),cutoff==as.integer(cutoff),cutoff>=1L,
      J_reference>=cutoff+2L,max_nodes>0,max_basis_mib>0)
    cells <- 2^(J_reference-1L)
    n <- cells-1L
    if (n>max_nodes || 8*n*cutoff/1024^2>max_basis_mib)
        stop("Reference mesh/basis exceeds its allocation guard.")
    h <- 2^-J_reference
    C <- Matrix::bandSparse(n,k=c(-1L,0L,1L),
      diagonals=list(rep(2*h/3,n-1L),rep(8*h/3,n),rep(2*h/3,n-1L)))
    x <- seq_len(n)*h
    E <- outer(x,2^seq_len(cutoff),function(x,k) sinpi(x*k))
    list(J=as.integer(J_reference),h=h,C=C,x=x,basis=E,cutoff=as.integer(cutoff))
}

rough_sampled_fem_basis <- function(J, reference_x) {
    stopifnot(length(J)==1L,is.finite(J),J==as.integer(J),J>=3L,J<=20L,
      all(is.finite(reference_x)),all(reference_x>0 & reference_x<.5))
    position <- reference_x*2^J
    left <- as.integer(floor(position))
    fraction <- position-left
    n <- 2^(J-1L)-1L
    rows <- seq_along(position)
    keep_left <- left>=1L & left<=n
    keep_right <- left+1L>=1L & left+1L<=n
    Matrix::drop0(Matrix::sparseMatrix(
      i=c(rows[keep_left],rows[keep_right]),
      j=c(left[keep_left],left[keep_right]+1L),
      x=c(1-fraction[keep_left],fraction[keep_right]),
      dims=c(length(position),n)))
}

rough_sampled_coefficients <- function(times,cutoff,alpha,diffusion=1,kappa=4) {
    stopifnot(all(is.finite(times)),all(times>=0),cutoff>=1L,cutoff==as.integer(cutoff))
    j <- seq_len(cutoff)
    rates <- rough_rates(j,alpha,diffusion,kappa)
    -expm1(-outer(rates,times))/(j*rates)
}

rough_compile_sampled_solver <- function(cache_dir=tempdir()) {
    target <- environment(rough_compile_sampled_solver)
    if (!exists("rough_sampled_evolve_cpp",envir=target,inherits=FALSE))
        Rcpp::sourceCpp(code=r"----(
// [[Rcpp::depends(RcppEigen)]]
// [[Rcpp::plugins(cpp11)]]
#include <RcppEigen.h>
#include <memory>
#include <vector>
#include <cmath>
using Eigen::VectorXd;
using Eigen::MatrixXd;
using Eigen::SparseMatrix;
typedef Eigen::SimplicialLDLT<SparseMatrix<double> > SampledFactor;

// Same shifted FEM update and sampled reference quadratic norm as the
// smooth notebook. No analytic time moments or continuum-tail sum enters.
// [[Rcpp::export]]
Rcpp::List rough_sampled_evolve_cpp(
    const Eigen::MappedSparseMatrix<double>& C_in, Rcpp::List matrices,
    Eigen::VectorXd residues, double remainder,
    Eigen::VectorXd load, Eigen::MatrixXd projection,
    const Eigen::MappedSparseMatrix<double>& metric_in,
    Eigen::MatrixXd residual_gram, Eigen::MatrixXd coefficients,
    Rcpp::IntegerVector target_steps, Eigen::VectorXd weights,
    double tau, int n_steps) {
  SparseMatrix<double> C=C_in, metric=metric_in;
  const int n=C.rows(), modes=projection.cols(), m=residues.size();
  const int samples=target_steps.size();
  if (n_steps<1 || tau<=0 || matrices.size()!=m || load.size()!=n ||
      projection.rows()!=n || coefficients.rows()!=modes ||
      coefficients.cols()!=samples || weights.size()!=samples ||
      residual_gram.rows()!=modes || residual_gram.cols()!=modes ||
      metric.rows()!=n || metric.cols()!=n)
    Rcpp::stop("Invalid sampled-reference dimensions.");
  SampledFactor mass_factor;
  mass_factor.compute(C);
  if (mass_factor.info()!=Eigen::Success) Rcpp::stop("Mass factorization failed.");
  std::vector<std::unique_ptr<SampledFactor> > factors;
  for (int i=0;i<m;++i) {
    SparseMatrix<double> A=Rcpp::as<SparseMatrix<double> >(matrices[i]);
    factors.emplace_back(new SampledFactor());
    factors.back()->compute(A);
    if (factors.back()->info()!=Eigen::Success) Rcpp::stop("Shifted factorization failed.");
  }
  VectorXd u=VectorXd::Zero(n),rhs(n),next(n),delta(n);
  int step=0;
  double total=0,compensation=0;
  for(int q=0;q<samples;++q) {
    if ((q & 4095)==0) Rcpp::checkUserInterrupt();
    int goal=target_steps[q];
    if(goal<step || goal>n_steps || goal<1 || !(weights[q]>0))
      Rcpp::stop("Invalid reference time-to-step mapping.");
    while(step<goal) {
      if ((step & 4095)==0) Rcpp::checkUserInterrupt();
      rhs.noalias()=C*u;
      rhs.noalias()+=tau*load;
      next=remainder*mass_factor.solve(rhs);
      for(int i=0;i<m;++i) next.noalias()+=residues[i]*factors[i]->solve(rhs);
      u.swap(next);
      ++step;
    }
    delta.noalias()=u-projection*coefficients.col(q);
    double value=weights[q]*(delta.dot(metric*delta)+
      coefficients.col(q).dot(residual_gram*coefficients.col(q)));
    if(!std::isfinite(value) || value<0) Rcpp::stop("Invalid sampled squared error.");
    double adjusted=value-compensation,updated=total+adjusted;
    compensation=(updated-total)-adjusted;
    total=updated;
  }
  if(step!=n_steps) Rcpp::stop("Reference grid did not reach the final solver step.");
  return Rcpp::List::create(Rcpp::Named("error")=std::sqrt(total),
    Rcpp::Named("sampled_squared")=total,Rcpp::Named("final_state")=u);
}
)----",env=target,cacheDir=cache_dir,showOutput=FALSE,verbose=FALSE)
    invisible(TRUE)
}

rough_sampled_error <- function(mesh,reference,error_projection,alpha,time_step,
    reference_time_step,m=14L,diffusion=1,T_final=1,
    max_solver_steps=1e7,max_reference_samples=2e6,max_coefficient_mib=512) {
    stopifnot(reference$cutoff>=mesh$J-1L,reference$J>mesh$J,
      length(T_final)==1L,is.finite(T_final),T_final>0,
      length(time_step)==1L,is.finite(time_step),time_step>0,
      length(reference_time_step)==1L,is.finite(reference_time_step),reference_time_step>0,
      length(max_solver_steps)==1L,is.finite(max_solver_steps),max_solver_steps>0,
      length(max_reference_samples)==1L,is.finite(max_reference_samples),max_reference_samples>0,
      length(max_coefficient_mib)==1L,is.finite(max_coefficient_mib),max_coefficient_mib>0)
    solver_N <- ceiling(T_final/time_step)
    reference_N <- ceiling(T_final/reference_time_step)
    if (!is.finite(solver_N) || !is.finite(reference_N) ||
        solver_N>max_solver_steps || reference_N>max_reference_samples ||
        8*reference_N*reference$cutoff/1024^2>max_coefficient_mib)
        stop("Time grid/reference coefficient matrix exceeds its allocation guard.")
    grid <- make_time_grid(T_final,time_step)
    reference_grid <- make_time_grid(T_final,reference_time_step)
    coefficients <- rough_sampled_coefficients(reference_grid$time_seq[-1L],
      reference$cutoff,alpha,diffusion,mesh$kappa)
    target_steps <- findInterval(reference_grid$time_seq[-1L],grid$time_seq,left.open=TRUE)
    op <- my.fractional.operators.frac(mesh$K,alpha/2,mesh$C,mesh$kappa^2,
      m,grid$time_step,diffusion)
    general <- function(A) as(as(A,"generalMatrix"),"CsparseMatrix")
    started <- proc.time()[["elapsed"]]
    result <- rough_sampled_evolve_cpp(general(mesh$C),
      lapply(op$partial_fraction_terms,general),op$residues,op$remainder,
      mesh$load,error_projection$projection,general(error_projection$metric),
      error_projection$residual_gram,coefficients,as.integer(target_steps),
      diff(reference_grid$time_seq),grid$time_step,grid$N)
    result$elapsed <- proc.time()[["elapsed"]]-started
    result$tau <- grid$time_step
    result$reference_tau <- reference_grid$time_step
    result$N <- grid$N
    result$reference_N <- reference_grid$N
    result
}
